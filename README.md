## Overview

**PROJECT DHWANI** is an indigenous, multi-terrain acoustic monitoring research initiative exploring how distributed sensing, signal processing, embedded intelligence, and edge AI can contribute to environmental awareness in geographically challenging environments.

The project investigates the integration of acoustic, seismic, and thermal sensing concepts with intelligent signal interpretation and distributed information processing.

Its central objective is to explore an adaptable monitoring architecture that can complement conventional observation systems in environments affected by terrain complexity, weather conditions, and limited visibility.

DHWANI 2.4 represents the current documented design iteration of this research project.

---

## The Problem

Conventional visual monitoring technologies can face limitations in environments involving:

- Dense vegetation and difficult terrain.
- Fog, rain, and adverse weather conditions.
- Remote geographical locations.
- Limited observation infrastructure.
- Environmental noise and complex signal conditions.

These challenges motivate the exploration of complementary sensing technologies that can provide additional environmental information.

---

## Proposed Research Approach

DHWANI explores a multi-layer sensing architecture integrating:

| Technology | Research Role |
|---|---|
| Acoustic Sensing | Environmental sound acquisition |
| Seismic Sensing | Ground-vibration information |
| Thermal Sensing | Complementary thermal observations |
| Signal Processing | Feature extraction and signal interpretation |
| Edge AI | Local intelligent signal analysis |
| Distributed Communication | Information exchange between system components |

The proposed architecture focuses on combining multiple information sources rather than relying on a single sensing modality.

---

## System Architecture

The DHWANI 2.4 technical concept is organized around several major subsystems.

### 1. Hawk-2 — Aerial Observation Concept

A conceptual aerial observation platform forming part of the overall monitoring architecture.

### 2. Panchatattva — Terrain-Specific Sensing Concepts

A family of five conceptual sensing variants designed around different environmental conditions:

- Maru — Desert
- Hima — Mountain
- Nadi — Riverine
- Vana — Jungle
- Sagara — Maritime

### 3. Fence Sentinel

A separate sensing concept for monitoring environmental and structural vibration patterns.

### 4. AI Brain

An edge-intelligence concept exploring signal processing, feature extraction, classification, and multi-modal information interpretation.

### 5. Mesh Communication Architecture

A distributed communication concept for transferring relevant information between system components.

*These descriptions represent the documented system design. They should not be interpreted as evidence that every subsystem has been constructed, integrated, or validated.*
